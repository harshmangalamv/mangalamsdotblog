Title: smoking is injurious
Slug: smoking-is-injurious
Description: smoking and fresh air both end in death, yet we know they are not the same. if the outcome is certain, why do we bother with what lies before it?

smoking is injurious. so is breathing fresh air!

sounds strange. both are eventually a mere part of the final outcome of life — (physical) death. it might appear true if you consider the fact that even if one follows all the rules and precautions, the eventual fate remains the same.

the nuance is that sharing an eventual outcome doesn't make two causes equivalent. smoking isn't merely correlated with worse health outcomes; there is strong causal evidence that it increases the risk of several serious diseases and premature death. breathing fresh air, on the other hand, helps the body function. it is true that bad air reduces life expectancy, but we are considering fresh air for our example.

and even if the eventual outcome is so certain, why should one bother optimizing what lies before it?

consider a machine that is functioning normally, with the boundary condition that some critical components will inevitably fail. the machine will eventually cease to function. one could maintain it, replace its parts, and extend its useful life. another could be left unattended and fail much sooner. both eventually reach the same endpoint.

but does that make the effort futile? we seem to think otherwise. we spend resources maintaining machines and considerable effort maintaining our bodies. we try to delay the inevitable, even when we know that we cannot prevent it.

perhaps the difference lies in what we are trying to preserve. more time could mean more experiences, more time with people we love, or simply more time to exist. perhaps we aren't really trying to avoid death as much as we are trying to hold on to everything that precedes it.

but then, why should any of this matter?

the universe doesn't particularly care whether a machine lasts ten years or a hundred. it doesn't reward a body for having been maintained well, nor does it promise that our precautions will save us from an untimely end. we care about these differences, but the universe offers no such assurance.

everything leads to death. the difference is in how soon, and perhaps in everything that happens before it.
