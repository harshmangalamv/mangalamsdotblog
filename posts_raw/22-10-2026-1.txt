Title: one and two
Description: earth is round, yet some people still believe it is flat. how many beliefs like that do we hold ourselves, and do they matter?

earth indeed is kind of spherical. an oblate spheroid, to be exact.

one, this is obvious to most who have had a basic education and have accepted it.

two, and then there are flat-earthers. their beliefs are intact enough that when [their own experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behind_the_Curve) showed the earth rotating, they doubted the experiment instead.

while two could arguably be called true until one was proven, i wonder how many such twos we cling to ourselves.

could this be because of the practical utility of the belief in question? what difference would it make if someone continued believing in the likes of two? take covid. in India itself, there were people who believed in unscientific ways of treating covid until almost everyone took the vaccine. if it were not a question of life and death, the choice would barely have mattered. while there can be subjectivity in choosing between two contrasting options, in most choices of opinion, one of the two is the truer.

choices made at a psychological level are often equally appealing. considering that our brains [thinking systems, to be more precise] are hard-wired for the path of least resistance, and that there are tonnes of decisions to make in a day, one barely knows of most of the decisions they have already taken without their knowledge, let alone choosing among them.

but then there must be a few choices that have lots of impact [20:80]. probably it is the buffer that takes care of the 80:20.
