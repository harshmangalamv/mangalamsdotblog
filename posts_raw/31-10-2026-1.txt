Title: voice
Slug: voice
Description: a person hugs a tree to stop the state from cutting it down. who is at fault, and whose meaning gets to count?

there is a person preventing the state from cutting down a tree by hugging it, much like in the Chipko Andolan. there are officers following orders to remove the obstacle to the project. they will take the person into custody for obstructing the state's cause.

who is at fault?

for the person, the tree is like a part of the family. they depend on it. the tree also holds meaning for them. we often talk about the things that are meaningful to us. but it seems that the weight of meaning depends on the representation of the group in question. if an act does not have enough of an audience, does it count as an act? apparently, it seems that the influential, policy-deciding group overshadows the significance of what matters to the other group.

what is the state's basic reason for existing? it exists to maintain a society that can sustain itself and prevent disorder. but if the very people it exists to serve are made to confront existential questions at the expense of profits for some other influential strata, how fair is that?

while some may debate the future benefits of the state's cause, arguing that it will bring improvements in xyz economic factors, that the common people's resistance is a motivated move, or that they will be compensated for their losses and should not be too emotional about the cause being pursued by the state, the question is: how far will the impact reach downstream? does this not eventually lead to distrust in the state? it will, for sure, make young people more vulnerable to being deprived of a proper education in such a rapidly changing world. this could result in a generation losing access to things that could have raised its standard of living and eventually contributed to the economy in ways whose effects would have lasted longer than the estimated benefits of the project. it is always more humane and civilised to preserve the person than to chase short-sighted data plots.

prosperity is multi-dimensional, and it is certainly not characterised by economics alone.
