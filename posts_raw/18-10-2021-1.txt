Title: Compounding
Date: 2021-10-18

<!-- First published on Blogspot: https://hmangalams.blogspot.com/2021/10/compounding.html -->
<!-- 2026-10-19: Einstein quote marked as attributed, with a note on its origin. -->

A line often attributed to Albert Einstein says, "Compound interest is the eighth wonder of the world. He who understands it, earns it … he who doesn't … pays it."[^einstein] Compounding plays so much important role invisibly, in sense of short term, that we often fail to recognize it.

Compounding in knowledge is apparent in "(S)he who aspires wisdom, achieves it." Learning little things continuously for longer times do compound. Every thing that's learned previously helps (and adds) in learning new things. Who can read a passage easily without learning the alphabets?

Like having the knowledge of alphabets and their simple addends help in reading simple words, which ultimately helps in reading of a sentence, and hence a paragraph! The same way simple addition-subtraction to Calculus, Economics etc.

Little things that we are doing un-consciously are a part of day to day activities, they reflect habits. When we are doing these little things consciously, we are doing a positive compounding.

Positive attitude, doing good, and speaking truth require (little) conscious efforts. Like treating people in good way in times when not in good mood. We seldom care of how to behave in similar scenarios, but if done consciously may lead to higher order (positive) effects - for for most of our deeds, we don't get any (significant) amount of positive returns. But our every action are telling the whole universe what we actually are.

Humans (the receivers of some of our actions) are emotional beings - they always remember how we made them feel, mostly unconsciously. So next time they interact with you, they'll seek the cue (on how to act next) and simultaneously act according to past interactions.

Ending with what Naval says, "Compound interest also happens in your reputation. If you have a sterling reputation and you keep building it for decades upon decades, people will notice. Your reputation will literally end up being thousands or tens of thousands of times more valuable than somebody else who was very talented but is not keeping the compound interest in reputation going."

[^einstein]: There is no evidence that Einstein said this. [Quote Investigator](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2019/09/09/interest/) finds the line credited to many people, from Baron Rothschild to John D. Rockefeller, and in a [related investigation](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2011/10/31/compound-interest/) calls an Einstein origin "very unlikely".
